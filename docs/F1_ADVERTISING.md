# How brands advertise at a Grand Prix (2025–2026)

Research notes behind the game's trackside brands (`src/world/partners.ts`). Real brand names appear
here **as references only**. Every brand in the game is fictional. Facts carry a source. Points marked
**[inference]** come from broadcast footage and photos, not from a written source.

## 1. Two kinds of partner

- **Series partners** are sold by Formula 1 itself. They appear at every race. In the 2025–26 Global
  Partner tier ([RTR Sports tracker, Oct 2026](https://rtrsports.com/en/blog/formula-1-sponsors-and-partners/)):

  | Category | Real partner |
  |---|---|
  | Energy | Aramco |
  | Cloud | AWS |
  | Logistics | DHL |
  | Beer | Heineken (0.0) |
  | Laptops / tech | Lenovo |
  | Luxury | LVMH: Louis Vuitton, Moët & Chandon, and TAG Heuer as Official Timekeeper from 2025 |
  | Cruise | MSC Cruises |
  | Tyres | Pirelli |
  | Airline | Qatar Airways |
  | CRM | Salesforce |

  The next tier down includes:
  - Crypto.com and Santander or American Express (payments).
  - PepsiCo from 2025: Gatorade, Doritos and Sting Energy. TV-visible trackside rights are part of its deal ([ESM](https://www.esmmagazine.com/a-brands/pepsico-seals-global-partnership-deal-with-formula-1-289234)).
  - KitKat, also with trackside branding ([F1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/formula-1-welcomes-kitkat-as-official-chocolate-bar-partner.78cQVrqIwT3KiU3j9xPkED)).
  - Paramount+, and Apple TV, which holds the US rights from 2026 ([Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2025/10/17/apple-acquires-f1-rights-in-us/)).

  Rolex was timekeeper from 2013 to 2024. TAG Heuer took over in 2025 under LVMH's 10-year deal ([F1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/tag-heuer-returns-to-formula-1-as-official-timekeeper-from-2025-season.5lS9YMOfwnuaQXkRGQW11v)). Heineken advertises on circuits and TV, never on cars ([Crash](https://www.crash.net/f1/news/231139/1/heineken-signs-f1-sponsorship-deal)).
- **Promoter (local) partners** are sold by the race organiser. Typical examples are the host's bank, telecom, beer, department store, tourism board, regional airline and ticketing platform. Madrid 2026 had Santander and Fever as "founding partners" plus El Corte Inglés ([Madring](https://www.madring.com/en/press-releases/santanter-founding-partner)). Mexico 2022 had Telcel, Infinitum, Claro, Heineken and Banorte ([Expansión](https://expansion.mx/empresas/2022/10/27/quien-financia-el-gp-de-mexico)).
- **Who sells what.** Historically FOM sold trackside advertising and hospitality while promoters kept ticket income. A few promoters bought trackside rights back: Monaco, Brazil, Singapore and Australia ([Paddock Magazine](https://paddockmagazine.com/?p=512)). That split dates from the Ecclestone era. F1 now sells "quality of exposure" (camera positions, LED rotation) rather than metres of fence ([Speedcafe 2023](https://www.speedcafe.com/?p=647429)). One contractor moves F1's own signage between races so it looks the same everywhere ([Radius case study](https://great.gov.uk/international/buy-from-the-uk/find-a-supplier/supplier/case-study/3950)).

## 2. The title partner names the race

Official titles read **FORMULA 1 \<BRAND\> \<RACE NAME\> \<YEAR\>**. The race name stays in the host's language. Titles for 2026 ([F1.com 2026 calendar](https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2026)):

| United States | Cruise | MSC Cruises |
| São Paulo | Cruise | MSC Cruises |

- In 2025 the titles were: Louis Vuitton (Australia), Lenovo (Japan and Hungary), Pirelli (Canada), MSC (Austria), Qatar Airways (Britain) ([F1.com 2025](https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2025)).
- Titles change hands almost every year. Australia has had four title partners in four years ([Forbes AU](https://www.forbes.com.au/?p=206955)).
- Middle East races are titled by the host state's own airline or company: Gulf Air, STC, Qatar Airways, Etihad.
- The title partner gets event artwork and "significant trackside placement", as TAG Heuer did at Monaco ([F1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/tag-heuer-becomes-first-title-partner-of-the-monaco-grand-prix.2H7KjYuyfv7tSQ24RH47wM.html)). Its branding runs "throughout" the circuit, for example Heineken 0.0 at Las Vegas ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/product-recommendations/lifestyle/formula-1-las-vegas-grand-prix-recap-2024-1235183305/)) and across Albert Park ([Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/?p=720506)).

## 3. Where brands appear

- **LED perimeter boards** run along the main straight and the grandstands. F1 names "LED rotational boards" as a way to sell the same position several times ([Speedcafe](https://www.speedcafe.com/?p=647429)). Silverstone runs about 70 LED screens ([LSI](https://lsionline.com/news/lighthouse-emerges-as-king-of-formula-one-c08xc3)). No source gives the rotation interval. **[inference]** Footage shows whole runs changing brand together every several seconds, so a brand "takes over" the shot.
- **Virtual boards.** Digitally replaced boards let F1 sell a board region by region ([SportBusiness 2019](https://tech.sportbusiness.com/2019/05/virtual-advertising-technology-unlocks-split-sponsorship-inventory-and-multi-lingual-graphics-for-formula-one/)). This is out of scope for the game.
- **Fixed vinyl boards** sit on barriers, tyre-wall belts, debris fences and posts behind run-offs. These are the long repeated runs of one brand in its colour block. **[inference]**
- **Painted run-off logos.**
  - The organiser supplies the artwork and a specialist contractor paints it in anti-slip paint ([Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/1851614988), [Hagerty](https://www.hagerty.co.uk/articles/videos-articles/what-does-it-take-to-paint-an-f1-track/)).
  - Logos sit on grass or tarmac run-off. Kerb logos are rare; Las Vegas's card-suit kerbs were the noted exception ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/even-the-las-vegas-f1-tracks-curbs-are-over-the-top)).
  - No source gives sizes in metres. **[inference]** Aerial and TV frames show them laid along the track, tens of metres long and several metres across. They read from the outside camera, so they are stretched along the direction of travel to compensate for the shallow viewing angle.
- **Timing partner.** Rolex had the oversized clock at pit exit and clocks around the circuit ([Máquina do Esporte](https://maquinadoesporte.com.br/?p=128315)). TAG Heuer clocks moved into the paddock in 2025 ([LVMH](https://www-v2.lvmh.com/en/news-lvmh/with-the-formula-1-tag-heuer-grand-prix-de-monaco-2025-lvmh-and-its-maisons-are-in-pole-position)). Gantry, podium, footbridge and fascia branding appear in footage but no source documents them. **[inference]** The start gantry carries the event's own title banner. The podium alternates the title partner with the series logo, and Louis Vuitton trunks hold the trophies ([Robb Report](https://robbreport.com.my/2025/02/27/louis-vuitton-and-formula-1-join-forces-in-a-decade-long-partnership/)). Each footbridge is sold to a single brand.
- **Scale.** Melbourne needs about 12 km of barriers and roughly 3,000 litres of paint for its signage ([IEDM](https://iedm.com.au/projects/formula-1-rolex-australian-grand-prix-2023), [Ben Waterworth](https://benwaterworth.com/post/building-the-australian-grand-prix)).

## 4. Venue character

- **Monza / Silverstone (2026).** The tyre maker is title partner. Its yellow and black runs take the main straight and the first-corner run-off.
- **Suzuka.** Honda owns the circuit and titled the race in 2022 ([Insider Sport](https://insidersport.com/2022/05/18/f1s-japanese-grand-prix-renamed-under-honda-sponsorship/)). The home car maker appears alongside the title partner (Aramco in 2026).
- **Spielberg.** The circuit owner, an energy-drink company, dresses its own track. The race title is still sold (Lenovo in 2026).
- **Las Vegas.** LED is everywhere, including the Sphere and grandstand screens ([SVG](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2023/11/16/sphere-and-formula-1-heineken-silver-las-vegas-grand-prix-announce-custom-race-content-for-exosphere/)). The game has no Las Vegas circuit.
- **Middle East.** The state airline titles the race. Light poles and LED-wrapped towers dominate the scene ([Sixteen-Nine](https://www.sixteen-nine.net/?p=21573)).
- **Mexico.** The race has no title partner. The promoter's local telecom, bank and beer fill the boards in the event's pink.

## 5. Rules the game follows

1. Each circuit has 20 brands. 14 are series partners present everywhere: timing, tyres, logistics, beer 0.0, champagne, energy drink, airline, bank, cloud, energy, luxury, crypto, cruise and laptops. The other 6 are local partners unique to the venue.
2. The title partner is a series partner, or the host's own airline in the Gulf. It gets:
   - the event's name on the gantry, the podium and the footbridge banners;
   - the most weight in every generic board run (roughly four contracts for each one a minor partner gets);
   - the main run-off paint.
3. Series partners take the camera-facing positions: the start gantry, the main-straight LED boards and the first-corner run-off. Local partners fill the grandstand fascias, the pit-building boards and the outer hoardings.
4. Each brand appears in its own colour block, in runs of consecutive boards (contracts) rather than scattered singles.
5. LED perimeter boards rotate their content in sync, so a run belongs to one brand at a time.
6. Painted run-off logos lie along the track on the outside of the corner, stretched along the direction of travel so they read from the TV camera.
